EV charging cost guide: home vs public vs petrol

What does it cost to charge an EV?

The cost of running an electric car depends almost entirely on where you charge. Charging at home overnight is dramatically cheaper than using public rapid chargers — and both are cleaner than petrol. Here's how the main options compare per mile, and over a typical 8,000-mile year:

How you chargePer milePer year*
Home, off-peak~2–3p~£190
Home, standard rate~7p~£560
Public rapid~13–16p~£1,160
Petrol car (~45mpg)~15p~£1,170

*Illustrative 2026 figures based on ~3.5 miles per kWh over 8,000 miles. Your costs depend on your car, tariff and driving.

Charging at home: the cheapest way

With a home charger on an off-peak or EV tariff, you can charge overnight for a fraction of daytime rates — often around 2–3p per mile. A smart charger schedules charging automatically to those cheap hours, so you wake up to a full battery having paid the lowest possible price.

For most drivers, the savings versus public charging and petrol mean a home charger pays for itself surprisingly quickly.

What about public charging?

Public rapid chargers are brilliant for longer trips, but they're the most expensive way to charge — typically 13–16p per mile, similar to or more than petrol. The trick most EV owners use is simple: charge at home for everyday driving, and only use public rapids when you're on the road.

Charging on solar: near-free miles

This is where it gets really cheap. If you have solar panels, a solar-compatible charger diverts surplus daytime energy straight into your car — so those miles cost close to nothing. Add a battery and you can store daytime sun to charge in the evening too.

Because an EV roughly doubles a typical home's electricity use, it's also one of the best reasons to get solar: the more energy you use, the faster a solar system pays back. You can estimate your solar savings with our savings calculator.

How much does a home charger cost?

A smart 7kW home charger, professionally fitted, typically costs from around £799. Three-phase 22kW units cost more but suit very few homes. With Spark you can browse chargers, see the fitted price and book your installation date entirely online.

Is home charging worth it?

For anyone who can charge at home, the answer is a clear yes. You'll typically cut your "fuel" costs by around 80% versus petrol, charge while you sleep, and never queue at a forecourt. Pair it with solar and your running costs drop close to zero — which is why so many EV owners go solar next.
