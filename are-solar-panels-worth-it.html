Are solar panels worth it in the UK?

The honest answer for most UK homes in 2026 is yes — but it depends on your roof, your usage and how you pay. Here's a straight look at the savings, the payback, and when solar isn't the right call.

Key takeaways

The short answer

For the large majority of UK homeowners, solar panels are worth it in 2026. With electricity prices sitting well above where they were a few years ago, a well-designed system delivers years of lower, more predictable bills and a return that compares favourably with leaving the same money in savings.

It is not universal, though. The value depends on your roof, how much electricity you use, and whether you add a battery — so it is worth understanding the moving parts before you commit.

What you actually save

A properly sized system typically cuts grid electricity use by up to 90%. You save in two ways: you stop buying expensive grid power during the day, and you get paid for surplus you export through the Smart Export Guarantee.

The exact figure depends on your tariff and how much energy you use while the sun is up. Add a battery and you shift far more of your own free daytime energy into the expensive evening peak — which is where most homes save the most.

How fast it pays back

Most home systems comfortably pay back their upfront cost within their 30-year lifespan, and often much sooner. Crucially, payback accelerates every time energy prices rise, because the electricity you generate is worth more.

What changes the answer

Your roof. South-facing is ideal, but east–west roofs still perform well by spreading generation across the day. Pitch and shading matter more than compass direction.

When you use energy. The more you can use (or store) during daylight, the better the return — though a battery largely solves the timing problem.

How you pay. Paying cash gives the strongest lifetime return, but finance can still leave you better off from day one if the monthly repayment is lower than your energy saving.

When it is not worth it

Solar makes less sense if your roof is heavily shaded for much of the day, if you only have a very small or north-facing roof with no alternative, or if you are planning to move within a year or two and won't see the payback (though solar can add to resale appeal). An MCS-certified installer will tell you honestly at survey if your home isn't a good fit.

Does a battery help?

For most homes, yes. A battery stores cheap daytime solar for use during the costly evening peak, which significantly raises how much of your own energy you actually use. Our battery storage guide covers sizing and payback in detail.
