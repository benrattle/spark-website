Commercial solar: costs, funding & payback

A straight-talking guide for UK businesses weighing up commercial solar in 2026 — what drives the cost, how PPA, purchase and finance compare, how payback really works, and the wider ESG and tax benefits.

Why businesses go solar

For most commercial energy users, electricity is a large and volatile overhead. On-site solar turns unused roof or land into decades of generation you control, cutting the volume you buy from the grid and insulating you from price spikes. Alongside the financial case, it delivers measurable carbon reductions that increasingly matter for tenders, ESG reporting and customer expectations.

What commercial solar costs

There's no single price — a commercial system is scoped to your roof or land, your energy use and your goals. The main cost drivers are system size (kWp), whether you add battery storage, the mounting type (rooftop, ground-mount or car-park canopy) and any grid-connection works. Larger systems generally cost less per kWp than domestic installs, improving the economics at scale.

The most accurate way to size and cost a system is from your half-hourly (HH) consumption data, which shows exactly when you use energy across the day.

PPA vs purchase vs finance

Outright purchase. You fund the system and keep all the savings and generation. This gives the strongest lifetime return and full ownership of the asset.

Finance or lease. You spread the cost over time. If the repayment is lower than the energy you save, you can be better off from day one while still moving toward ownership.

Power Purchase Agreement (PPA). A funder pays for and owns the system, and you simply buy the electricity it generates at an agreed rate — usually below grid prices — with no upfront capital. It's a popular route for businesses that want the savings and carbon benefits without the capital outlay.

How payback works

Payback is driven less by how much you generate and more by how much of that generation you use on site. Electricity you consume directly displaces expensive grid power; surplus you export earns far less. Businesses with strong daytime demand — warehouses, manufacturing, retail, offices — tend to see the best returns, and battery storage can shift more generation into the hours you actually need it.

ESG & tax benefits

Beyond the bill, on-site solar directly reduces your Scope 2 emissions, giving you hard numbers for ESG, SECR and tender responses — supported by live generation reporting. Solar assets may also qualify for capital allowances, letting many businesses offset part of the investment against taxable profits (your accountant will confirm what applies). Pairing solar with commercial EV charging extends the benefit to your fleet and staff parking.
