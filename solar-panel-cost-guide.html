How much do solar panels cost in 2026?

What does a solar system cost in 2026?

As a rough guide, a fully-fitted home solar system in the UK costs between £5,000 and £13,000 in 2026, depending mainly on the size of the system your roof and energy use call for. Here's how that typically breaks down by home size:

Home sizeSystemTypical cost
1–2 bed~3.5 kWp£5,000–£7,000
3 bed (semi)~5 kWp£7,000–£9,500
4+ bed (detached)~6–8 kWp£9,500–£13,000

Prices are fully-fitted, illustrative ranges for 2026 and include standard installation. Your exact price depends on your roof and system design.

What affects the price?

Two systems on two different homes can cost quite different amounts. The biggest factors are:

System size. More panels generate more electricity but cost more upfront. The right size balances your roof space, your energy use and your budget.

Panel quality. Premium panels (like the AIKO panels we fit as standard) generate more from the same roof and last longer — usually worth it over a 30-year lifespan.

Battery storage. Optional, but it transforms how much of your own free energy you actually use. More on that below.

Your roof. Roof type, pitch, access and whether scaffolding is needed all play a part in the installation cost.

How much does adding a battery cost?

A home battery typically adds £2,500 to £5,000 depending on capacity. Without one, you use solar only as it's generated and sell the rest back cheaply. With one, you store cheap daytime energy and use it in the expensive evening peak — so you buy far less from the grid.

For most households a battery meaningfully increases self-consumption (the share of your own solar you actually use), which is why the majority of our customers choose to include one.

Payback and lifetime savings

A well-designed system cuts grid electricity costs by up to 90%, and the Smart Export Guarantee pays you for surplus energy you sell back. Most systems comfortably pay back their cost within their 30-year lifespan — and faster every time energy prices rise, because the value of the energy you generate goes up with them.

You can put your own numbers in with our savings calculator to see an estimate for your home.

Ways to pay

You don't have to pay upfront. Spark offers several routes to spread the cost: interest-free credit over 5 years, longer interest-bearing credit over 5, 10 or 15 years, and a solar lease over up to 25 years. On a longer term, the monthly repayment can be lower than the amount you save on energy — so you're better off from the day the system switches on.

See the full breakdown on our Savings & Finance page.

So, is solar worth it in 2026?

For most UK homeowners, yes. With energy prices structurally higher than they were a few years ago, solar and battery offer years of lower, more predictable bills, a return that compares well to leaving money in savings, and a meaningful cut to your carbon footprint. The key is a quality system, properly sized, with savings you can trust — which is exactly what an independently-validated Spark quote gives you.
