Is my roof suitable for solar panels?

Most UK homes are well suited to solar — but a few things make a difference to how much you'll generate. Here's what surveyors actually look at, and why direction matters less than people think.

Direction and pitch

South-facing roofs generate the most over a year, but this matters far less than people assume. East and west-facing roofs still perform very well — often 80–90% of a south-facing roof — and they spread generation across morning and evening, which can actually suit your usage better. A pitch of roughly 30–40 degrees is ideal, but a wide range works well.

Shading

Shade from chimneys, trees, aerials or neighbouring buildings reduces output, but it rarely rules solar out. Modern systems use panel-level electronics (optimisers or microinverters) so a shaded panel doesn't drag down the rest, and a good designer arranges the layout to avoid the worst-affected areas. Our survey assesses shading across the day and seasons.

Roof type and condition

Almost every roof type is suitable — tile, slate and flat roofs are all routinely fitted with solar, each with the right mounting method. The main thing surveyors check is condition: your roof should be sound enough to carry panels for decades. If it needs minor work first, we'll flag it. Panels are lightweight and don't typically need structural changes on a healthy roof.

Space and system size

The more usable roof area you have, the larger the system you can fit — but you don't need a huge roof to benefit. A typical home system uses around 9–16 panels, and even a modest roof can deliver meaningful savings. Where roof space is limited, premium high-output panels (which we fit as standard) generate more from each panel, making the most of what you have.
